Operations panel and API for appointments, patients (holders and dependents), plans and the professionals' agenda.

## How scheduling works

**Price list and discounts.** Clinics, specialties and professionals carry their prices. An appointment is priced when it is saved: gross = `clinic fee + specialty price + professional fee` (using the professional's clinic and specialty), minus the **discount of the customer's plan**: each plan has a default `discount_percent` and optional per-specialty overrides (`PUT /modalities/{id}/discounts`). The appointment stores gross, percentage, discount and final amount, plus the plan and the user who booked it. Everything is a snapshot: later price or discount changes do not alter existing appointments; changing the appointment's professional or customer recalculates it. `GET /appointments/quote?customer_id&professional_id&on=YYYY-MM-DD[&appointment_id]` previews the price for a date. The client never sends amounts. Pricing lives in `api/app/pricing.py`.

**On-call services.** A professional can be **available for on-call** (`on_call_enabled`): besides the regular working hours it then has **on-call shifts** (`on_call_hours`, weekly ranges like the working hours, never overlapping them) and an **on-call rate** (`on_call_price_cents`, validated **greater** than the regular fee). An appointment booked in an on-call slot is an on-call service (`on_call=true` on the appointment and on the agenda's slots) priced with the **on-call price list**: the professional's on-call rate, the clinic's `on_call_fee_cents` and the specialty's `on_call_price_cents` (both optional and never below the regular value; unset means the regular one). The plan discount and the appointment limit apply as usual, and the professional's payout is the on-call rate snapshotted on the appointment. All on-call values are edited like any other price (professional, clinic and specialty forms; `PUT /professionals/{id}/working-hours` also takes `on_call_ranges`) and take effect on the next bookings: moving an appointment between a regular and an on-call slot re-prices it, moving it within the same kind keeps its snapshot. Disabling on-call availability removes the shifts. `GET /appointments/quote?...&at=YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm` previews the price of a slot (regular or on-call). Rules live in `api/app/pricing.py` (`price_components`) and `api/app/scheduling.py` (`on_call_slot`).

**Urgent visits.** An **ad-hoc urgent visit** is a request outside the agenda. A professional takes them only if it says so **in its own profile** (`GET /professionals/me`, `PUT /professionals/me/urgent` with `urgent_enabled` and its **urgent rate** `urgent_price_cents`, validated at **least three times the regular fee** — omitted when enabling, the current rate or that minimum is used; administrators see and edit the same fields on the professional form, under the same rule). An urgent visit is booked with `urgent: true` on the appointment (staff or the client; omitted on edit, the kind of visit stays as it is) at **any time** — inside or outside the working hours, on or off the slot grid — **except during the professional's on-call shifts, where urgent visits are never accepted** (`409`); a professional who does not offer them is `400`. Time off, the conflict rules and the capacity still apply. It is priced with the professional's urgent rate in place of the fee, the regular clinic fee and specialty price, and the plan discount and appointment limit as usual; the professional's payout is the urgent rate. Switching the flag on an appointment re-prices it; a signed one is frozen. The agenda lists urgent visits even outside the hours (`urgent` on the slot), the quote takes `urgent=true`, and the reports count them (`urgent`, `urgent_cents`; the payout rows carry the professional's current `urgent_fee_cents` and the urgent totals, the statement/CSV flag the lines). Rules live in `api/app/scheduling.py` (`assert_urgent_allowed`) and `api/app/pricing.py` (`urgent_minimum`, `price_components`).

**Remote appointments.** In person is the default; a professional may also **offer remote appointments** (video call): `remote_enabled` in its own profile (`PUT /professionals/me/remote` with `remote_enabled`, an optional **remote rate** `remote_price_cents` — omitted keeps the current one, `same_as_fee: true` drops it so remote appointments pay the regular fee — and `GET /professionals/me` shows the rate in force as `remote_fee_cents`); administrators and supervisors see and edit the same fields on the professional form. A remote appointment is booked with `remote: true` (staff or the client; omitted on edit, the kind stays as it is) **in a regular slot of the agenda, exactly like an in-person one**: working hours, time off, the conflict rules, the capacity, the plan and its allowance all apply. It is refused inside the professional's on-call shifts (`409`) and can never be an urgent visit (`400`); a professional who does not offer them is `400`. It is priced with the **remote price list**: the professional's remote rate, the clinic's `remote_fee_cents` and the specialty's `remote_price_cents` (both optional, unset means the regular value; a clinic may charge 0 since no room is used), the plan discount and the appointment limit as usual; the professional's payout is the remote rate snapshotted on the appointment. Every remote appointment carries a **meeting link** (`meeting_url`): a unique room issued when it is booked as remote — under `REMOTE_MEETING_BASE_URL`, default the public Jitsi Meet service — unless the staff sends its own link in the body (`meeting_url`, e.g. the professional's Meet/Zoom room; omitted or empty keeps the current one or issues one; a client sending one gets `403`). Switching an appointment between in person and remote re-prices it and issues or drops the link (and notifies the patient); moving a remote appointment keeps both; a signed one is frozen. The patient, the professional and the staff see the link on the appointment, and every booking, change, cancellation and reminder message carries it in place of the clinic's address and map. The attendance trail is unchanged: the patient checks in when joining the call, reads the virtual card code to the professional to start the visit, the check-out and the signature follow. The agenda flags remote bookings (`remote` on the slot), the quote takes `remote=true`, and the reports count them (`remote`, `remote_cents`; the payout rows carry the professional's current `remote_fee_cents` and the remote totals, the statement/CSV flag the lines). Rules live in `api/app/remote.py` (meeting link), `api/app/scheduling.py` (`assert_remote_allowed`) and `api/app/pricing.py` (`price_components`).

**Appointment limit.** A plan covers a number of appointments per period: `appointment_limit` (0 = unlimited) per `appointment_limit_period` — `month` (each monthly billing period of the subscription, the same periods as the installments, anchored on the join day) or `subscription` (the whole period). The allowance belongs to the holder's subscription, so the holder and the dependents share it. **It never blocks scheduling**: an appointment booked once the allowance of its period is used up is accepted but **pays the full price** (no discount) and is flagged `over_limit`, with the limit snapshotted on it (`plan_limit`, `plan_limit_period`). What takes a place: scheduled and completed appointments priced with the plan and inside the limit; cancelled ones give their place back and over-limit ones never took one. The decision is a snapshot like the prices — it is re-evaluated whenever the appointment is (re)booked (create, change of patient or day, re-activation; then the price-list snapshot is kept and only the plan side is recomputed), so cancelling an earlier appointment does not retroactively discount a later one unless that one is edited. The quote reports the allowance of the date's period (`plan_used`, `plan_remaining`, `plan_window_start/end`, `over_limit`), the subscription reports the usage of the current period and per installment (`appointments_used`), and the forms show it (the appointment form warns when the slot picked will pay the full price). Rules live in `api/app/usage.py`.

**Service capacity.** Professionals and clinics also serve patients outside the network, so each one can reserve only part of its agenda for Santé Saúde: a maximum number of appointments **per day** (`daily_limit`) and **per calendar month** (`monthly_limit`); 0 means unlimited. The clinic's capacity is shared by every professional working there and applies on top of each professional's own. Unlike the plan's appointment limit, capacity is a **hard stop**: once a day or month is at the limit the system refuses to book another appointment there (`409` naming who is full and the count). Scheduled and completed appointments fill capacity; cancelled ones give their place back and cancelling is always allowed. Capacity is checked when an appointment is (re)booked — created, or edited onto another professional or day, or re-activated — never when notes are edited, the time moves within the same day or the visit is signed, so lowering a limit below what is already booked never blocks record keeping: existing appointments stand and only new bookings are refused. Rules live in `api/app/capacity.py`.

**Working hours.** Each professional has recurring weekly hour ranges (e.g. Monday 08:00–12:00 and 13:00–17:00) and a slot duration (default 30 min). Ranges are cut into slots; each slot takes one appointment. On-call shifts are ranges of the same kind flagged `on_call` (see above). A professional created without hours receives the default week (`GET /professionals/default-week`: Mon–Fri, 08:00–12:00 / 13:00–17:00). Hours are edited with the professional (`working_hours` in `POST/PUT /professionals`) or via `PUT /professionals/{id}/working-hours`.

**Time off.** Dates (whole day) or date + `HH:mm` range the professional does not want to work: `GET/POST /professionals/{id}/time-off`, `DELETE /professionals/{id}/time-off/{offId}`.

**Agenda.** `GET /professionals/{id}/slots?start=YYYY-MM-DD&end=YYYY-MM-DD` (up to 62 days) returns each day's slots as `available`, `booked` (with appointment id and patient name, and `urgent` / `remote` for the kind of visit), `blocked` (time off) or `full` (free, but the day is at the professional's or the clinic's capacity — the day also carries `full` / `full_reason`), each with `on_call` when it belongs to an on-call shift. `exclude_appointment` leaves the appointment being moved out of the capacity counts, matching the edit rule. The front-end renders it as a week calendar: in the appointment form the user clicks a free slot (full days are marked and not clickable); in the professional's agenda page staff can see availability and click a slot to mark it as time off.

**Rules on create/edit of an appointment** (`400`/`409` with a message):

- the time must be a slot of the professional for that weekday and not inside a time off;
- the professional cannot have two appointments in the same slot;
- the patient (holder, or the dependent when one is set) cannot be with another professional in the same slot;
- the professional's and the clinic's service capacity for the day and the month must not be full (see above);
- a remote appointment needs a professional who offers them, a regular slot (never an on-call shift) and is never urgent.

`agendada` and `realizada` occupy a slot. `cancelada` does not occupy one and is never blocked, so an appointment can always be cancelled even after the hours change. Times are `HH:mm`; seconds and offsets are dropped. Rules live in `api/app/scheduling.py`.

**Plans are mandatory.** A customer needs an **active** plan subscription to schedule for themselves or a dependent. A subscription has a `joined_on` and a required `expires_on`, and is **billed monthly**: one installment per month of the period (due on the join day of each month) is generated automatically; the amount is the plan value plus the extra-dependent price for every dependent above the plan's limit, minus the special discount of the seller's region when a seller sold it (see *Sellers and sales regions*) or the negotiated discount of a corporate agreement, snapshotted on the enrollment (see *Corporate partnerships (B2B)*); unpaid installments are re-priced when dependents change. Payments are registered with `PUT /subscriptions/{id}/installments/{installmentId}` (`{"paid_on": "YYYY-MM-DD", "paid_cents": optional}`; `paid_on: null` undoes).

The subscription **status is derived**, never typed: `cancelada` (cancelled) → `migrada` (replaced by a plan upgrade and past its end) / `vencida` (past `expires_on`) → `pendente` (first installment unpaid) → `inadimplente` (an installment past due) → `ativa`. A customer has at most one subscription in force at a time — subscriptions may not overlap: a new one covering days of the current one either upgrades it (see *Plan upgrade*) or is refused, while one starting after the current one ends is simply the next plan (`POST /subscriptions/{id}/cancel`, `/reactivate`; `GET /subscriptions/customer/{customerId}` returns the one in force today).

Booking an appointment (create, or edit that changes patient/day or re-activates a cancelled one) is refused with `409` unless the plan is `ativa`, the appointment date is inside `[joined_on, expires_on]`, and the plan's waiting period (`grace_days` after `joined_on`) is over. Editing notes or marking a past appointment as completed does not re-check, so a plan that lapsed later does not block record keeping. Rules live in `api/app/billing.py`.

**Plan upgrade.** A customer with a current (non-cancelled) subscription cannot simply get another one (`409`): the new subscription **upgrades** it — `POST /subscriptions` with `upgrade: true` (the staff, the client for its own plan, or a seller as a sale of its region like any other). The new plan must start after the current one's join date; the current plan then **ends the day before** the new one starts (`expires_on`), its open installments after that are dropped and the paid ones stay (no proration; ending before a paid installment *starts* is refused — start the new plan after it), and the two records point at each other (`upgraded_from_id` / `upgraded_to_id`; the replaced one shows the status **`migrada`** once past its end). Until the new plan starts, the replaced one is still the customer's current plan (`GET /subscriptions/customer/{id}` returns the plan in force today) and covers appointments; from then on only the new one does, once its first installment is paid. Editing either side keeps them apart (the old cannot run past the new one's start, the new cannot start before the old ends). The customer is notified (`subscription.upgraded`), and the staff when a client or a seller did it. The UI offers it on the subscription form ("Upgrade plan", with the current plan shown) and to the client as "Upgrade my plan".

**Patients.** Holders and dependents share the same record: birth date (required for dependents), sex, CPF, RG, CNS, contact, mother's/father's name, address, blood type, allergies and notes; dependents also have the kinship.

## Sellers and sales regions

Plans can be sold by **sellers**, and **every seller belongs to exactly one sales region** (`/regions`, `/sellers`; UI under "Sales"). The region holds the rules its sellers sell under:

* **Commission** (`commission_bp`, basis points: 1250 = 12.5 %): what the seller earns on the plan fees **actually received** from its sales — each paid installment pays `paid_cents × rate`, rounded half up.
* **Scope** (`areas`): where its sellers may sell — whole states (`{"state": "SP"}`) and/or single municipalities (`{"state": "RJ", "city": "Niterói"}`); at least one. The **customer's address** (the holder's state and city) must be inside it; cities are compared ignoring case, accents and extra spaces.
* **Plans available** (`plans`): the only plans its sellers may sell, each with the region's **special discount** on the monthly fee (`discount_percent`, 0 = list price). The discount comes off the whole monthly amount (plan value + extra dependents) of every installment.

A subscription records the sale with `seller_id` (optional: without it the plan was sold directly, at the counter or by the client, and no region applies). **A seller cannot sell outside its region**: `POST /subscriptions` (or an edit that changes the seller, the plan or the customer) is refused with `409` when the plan is not offered in the seller's region, the customer's address is outside the region's scope or has no city/state to check, or the seller is inactive. A valid sale **snapshots** the region, its commission and its discount on the subscription (`region_id`, `commission_bp`, `discount_percent`; each installment carries the `discount_cents` it granted), so later changes to the region — a new rate, a plan dropped, the scope narrowed, the seller moved to another region — never rewrite existing sales; they apply to the next ones. Editing only the dates keeps the terms sold; re-activating a cancelled subscription restores it as sold. On edit, omitting `seller_id` keeps the seller and `null` removes it (open installments go back to the list price; paid ones are never touched).

Only the staff attributes a sale to a seller (a client sending `seller_id` gets `403`), and the commission is internal: clients see who sold their plan and their discount, never the commission (`null` in their responses). `GET /subscriptions/quote?customer_id&modality_id[&seller_id]` previews a sale (list and discounted monthly amount, commission) and says whether the seller's region allows it (`allowed`, `reason`) — the subscription form uses it to offer only the seller's plans and to block an out-of-region sale before saving. `GET /subscriptions?seller_id=|region_id=` lists a seller's or a region's sales.

Regions and sellers are read by the staff and written by administrators and supervisors (commission and discounts move money, like the price list). A region with sellers or with sales cannot be deleted; a seller with sales is deactivated instead of deleted (it keeps its history and cannot sell). Rules live in `api/app/sales.py`.

**Seller logins.** A seller can sign in: an administrator creates a user with the role `seller` linked to the seller record (`/users`, like professional logins; the welcome message names its region). The login opens the **seller's own area** ("Sales area" in the UI) and nothing else — no appointments, catalog management, other sellers or regions, reports or payouts (`403`):

* **My sales** (`/subscriptions`, scoped): the plans it sold. A seller records sales as itself (`POST /subscriptions`; a `seller_id` of someone else is `403`, omitted or null means itself) under the rules of its region — **to anyone whose address is inside the region's scope**, whoever registered the customer; there is no other limit on whom it sells to. It follows its sales (status, installments, the commission of each payment) and registers no payments — the staff does.
* **Changing or cancelling a sale needs the customer's confirmation.** A seller does not edit or cancel a subscription directly (`PUT`/`cancel` are `403` for it); it opens a **change request** — `POST /subscriptions/{id}/changes` with `kind: "edit"` (new `modality_id`, `joined_on` and/or `expires_on`; omitted fields keep their value) or `kind: "cancel"` (`cancelled_on`, default today) and an optional `note` shown to the customer. The request is checked at once (region rules, paid installments) but **nothing changes until confirmed**: the customer is notified (in-app and by email) and the staff too; the customer's own login confirms or rejects it (`POST …/changes/{cid}/confirm` | `/reject`, optional `note`), or the staff does it **recording the customer's answer** given in person — the seller never decides its own request. Confirming applies the same rules as a staff edit/cancel and keeps the terms sold; the seller is told of the decision. One pending request per subscription; the seller may withdraw it (`DELETE …/changes/{cid}`); `GET …/changes` lists the history; the subscription carries `pending_change`. Model `SaleChange`, table `sale_changes`.
* **Customers** (`/customers`): every customer record — it registers new customers (`customers.seller_id` remembers who did), edits them and their dependents; it never deletes a customer or touches virtual cards.
* **My region** (`GET /sellers/me`): its own record with the rules of its region (commission, scope, plans and special discounts).
* **My commissions**: `GET /reports/commissions` and `/reports/commissions/{ownId}` scoped to itself (another seller is `403`) — per period, plus its **portfolio today** (`portfolio`: customers it registered or sold to, subscriptions by status, monthly fees of the active ones, installments due in 7 days and overdue) so it knows whom to remind.
* **My account** (`PUT /sellers/me`: document, contact email, phone — name, region and status stay the staff's; `PUT /auth/password` with the current and the new password, available to every role).
* **Home** (`GET /dashboard`): sales this month and in total, customers, active / awaiting first payment / overdue plans, installments due in 7 days, commission this month and in total, the recent sales.

**Teams: sellers, coordinators, regional managers.** Sellers are organised in teams of **any depth** inside a region: a seller **reports to a leader** (`sellers.leader_id`, a seller of the **same region**; no cycles — a seller cannot report to itself or to anyone below it), who may report to a regional manager, and so on. A seller leading others cannot leave its team's region and cannot be deleted until its team is reassigned. The **override rates are the region's**, set by the company per level above the seller who sold (`regions.override_bps`, basis points, up to five levels: `[300, 100]` = 3 % to the direct leader, 1 % to the leader's leader; levels beyond the list earn nothing): each ancestor earns its level's rate, **on top of** the seller's commission and paid by the company, on every plan fee received from the sale. Like the commission, the ancestors and their rates are **snapshotted on the sale** (`sale_overrides`: level, seller, rate): moving a seller to another team, changing the region's rates or deactivating an ancestor later never rewrites what a sale pays (an inactive ancestor gets no row on *new* sales; the levels above it still do). The staff sets teams up on the seller record (`/sellers`: "Reports to"; `GET /sellers/{id}/team` lists the direct reports) and the rates on the region; both sides are notified when a seller joins or leaves a team (`seller.leader_changed`), and every ancestor with a row when a seller below records a sale (`team.sale_recorded`, with its level and rate) and on every override earned (`override.earned`).

A leader's login sees, besides its own area, **My team** (`/my-team`): every seller below it (all levels, with the level and who each reports to), their sales, payments and commission in the period and what their sales paid their leaders, the organisation's totals per period, and their sales in "My sales" (read-only: only the selling seller asks for changes). `GET /sellers/me` carries `leader`, `team` (direct reports), `organisation` (everyone below, with `depth`) and the region's `override_bps`; `/auth/me` carries `seller_team_size` (everyone below). The commission report and statement take the team into account:

* `GET /reports/commissions` — every seller row has both sides: its own sales (`sold`, `payments`, `revenue_cents`, `commission_cents`) and the sales below it (`team_size` direct, `organisation_size` all levels, `team_sold`, `team_payments`, `team_revenue_cents`, `override_cents` at the rate snapshotted for its level), `leader_override_cents` (what its own sales paid its ancestors, all levels) and `total_cents` (commission + override); rows are ordered by total earnings. The totals are the **company's cost** (every commission and every override, `total_cents`) — except in one seller's report (`seller_id`, or a seller login), where they are **that seller's earnings**: its commission on its own sales plus its override on the sales below it. A leader's login sees itself and everyone below it (`seller_id` of one of them is allowed; anyone else is `403`); a seller leading nobody sees itself only.
* `GET /reports/commissions/{sellerId}` — the statement lists every payment that pays the seller something: `kind: commission` lines (its own sales) and `kind: override` lines (sales below it, with `sold_by` and the `level`), each with the rate and amount; `commission_cents`, `override_cents` and `total_cents` sum them.
* The seller's home carries `team_size` (everyone below), `team_sales_month`, `override_month_cents`, `override_total_cents` (and `leader_name` for a seller reporting to someone), and the recent sales include the team's (`seller_name`).

**Deactivating a seller revokes its access at once**: the login is refused and tokens already issued stop working on the next request (`401 "Your seller access was deactivated; contact the company"` — the UI sends it back to the login page with that message). No sale can be attributed to it any more (`409`); its sales and the commissions still due stay on the books (the commission report lists it as inactive) for the company to settle. Reactivating restores the access. The staff is notified when a seller records a sale on its own.

**Commission report** (admin): `GET /reports/commissions` (`granularity`, `start`, `end`, `region_id`, `seller_id`) — plans sold by sellers (by sale date), cancelled, payments and fees received (by payment date), special discounts granted and the commission due, per period, per seller (every seller of the filter, also those with nothing due) and per region of the sale; `GET /reports/commissions/{sellerId}?start&end` is the statement backing the payment (one line per installment paid, with the rate snapshotted on the sale). UI: Reports → "Seller commissions".

## Plan categories and enterprise plans

Plans may be grouped in **categories** (`/plan-categories`: name, description, **enterprise**, waiting period; UI "Records → Plan categories"; read by the network, written by administrators and supervisors — a category with plans cannot be deleted; names are unique whatever their case). A plan belongs to at most one (`category_id` on `/modalities`; the plan carries `category_name`, `enterprise` and `effective_grace_days`); plans without one behave as before.

* **Enterprise plans.** The plans of an **enterprise** category are only available through **company agreements** (see *Corporate partnerships*): subscribing one without an agreement — the staff, a client on its own, an upgrade — is refused (`409`), and so is taking an enrollment on one out of its agreement; a sales region cannot offer one and a seller can never sell one (nor ask for it in a change request). The quote says so (`allowed: false`), the subscription form disables them unless an agreement is chosen, and clients and sellers never see them. Their special discounts and conditions are the plan's own — the appointment discount and its per-specialty overrides, the allowance, the dependents — plus what each agreement negotiates (the fee discount, the split, its own waiting period). A plan cannot move to an enterprise category, nor a category become enterprise, while individuals have one of its plans in force or a sales region offers it (`409`, with the counts): sales already made are never stranded.
* **The category's waiting period.** A category may set the waiting period of all its plans (`grace_days`; blank: each plan's own): it then replaces each plan's `grace_days` — e.g. the Enterprise category with no waiting period. The waiting period of a subscription is, from the most specific: the one its company agreement negotiated for the plan, the category's, the plan's. Like the plan's, it is read when an appointment is booked (a change applies to the next bookings). Rules live in `api/app/categories.py`.

`GET /reports/plans` carries each plan's category (`category_name`, `enterprise`) and a `by_category` breakdown (plans, sold, cancelled, payments, revenue, appointments covered / over the limit).

## Corporate partnerships (B2B)

Besides the plans sold to final clients (at the counter, by the client itself or by a seller), plans can be **negotiated directly with companies** for their employees. A **partner company** (`/partners`: name, CNPJ, HR contact and its email, city/state, active) signs **agreements** (`/agreements`): **six-month contracts** (`starts_on`; `ends_on` is derived — six months less a day), each with:

* **Plans and conditions** (`plans`): the only plans its employees may be enrolled in, each with a **discount on the monthly fee** (`discount_percent`, off the plan value plus extra dependents, rounded half up like every discount) and optionally a **waiting period** of its own (`grace_days`; `null` = the plan's, `0` = none).
* **A minimum of employees** (`min_employees`) the company commits to in exchange of those conditions.
* **Who pays** (`payer`): `company` — the company pays every fee, in one **invoice per month** —, `shared` — a **co-payment**: each fee is split between the company and the employee (see *Co-payment* below) — or `employee` — each employee pays its own fees at the negotiated price.

**Frozen terms.** The employees **always** get the negotiated conditions: the discount is snapshotted on each enrollment (the subscription's `discount_percent`, like a seller's sale) and the agreement's terms — partner, start, minimum, payer, plans and conditions — **cannot change once an employee is enrolled** (`terms_frozen`; `409`): only the name, the notes and the activity do. A renegotiation is the **next contract**: `POST /agreements/{id}/renew` creates it from the day after the current one ends, with the same terms unless new ones are given (`name`, `min_employees`, `payer`, `plans`, `notes`), and with `carry_employees` (default) enrolls in it every employee enrolled until the end of the current contract (in the same plan, when the new contract still covers it). Renew without the employees to adjust the new terms before anyone is enrolled.

**The minimum and the shortfall.** The headcount is measured **per month**: the employees (holders; dependents do not count; an employee counts once) with a fee of the agreement **due in that month**. For every month billed to fewer employees than the minimum, **the company pays Santé Saúde the difference** — the discounts granted on that month's fees (the **shortfall**). The shortfall is projected while the month runs (the headcount may still change) and charged once it ended, due on the 10th of the next month. Its payment is recorded with the amount and the headcount of that moment (`agreement_charges`, taxed like plan fees; undo with `DELETE /agreements/{id}/invoices/{period}/shortfall`), so later changes to the month never rewrite it. So, below the minimum, Santé Saúde still receives the list price; at or above it, the company and its employees get the conditions.

**Enrollment.** An employee is enrolled with an ordinary subscription carrying the agreement — `POST /subscriptions` with `agreement_id` (the staff), or the company itself in its portal (below) — so coverage, the appointment allowance, upgrades, taxes and reports work as for any plan. The enrollment must use a plan the agreement covers and fit inside its six months; a new one needs the agreement and its partner active and the agreement not ended (`409`). It is **never a seller's sale** (no region, no commission; `seller_id` with `agreement_id` is refused). A client or a seller cannot enroll anyone (`403`). On edit, omitting `agreement_id` keeps it, `null` takes the employee out (open fees back to the list price), another id moves it (checked as a new enrollment). The employee's client login sees its corporate plan (`partner_name`, `agreement_name`, `paid_by_company`) but not the company's headcount (`null`); changes, cancellation and upgrades go through its company (`403`).

**One plan at a time means no overlap.** A customer may not have two non-cancelled subscriptions covering the same days; a plan starting **after** the current one ends is simply the next one (no upgrade needed — this is how employees are enrolled in the next contract while the current one runs), and an upgrade only replaces a plan that overlaps the new one. (Before, a customer whose plan had ended could not get a new one without `upgrade`, which stretched the ended plan up to the new one, charging the months in between.)

**The company's invoices.** `GET /agreements/{id}/invoices` gives one invoice per month with fees due: employees billed against the minimum (`employees`, `min_employees`, `below_minimum`), the fees (`amount_cents`, `discount_cents`, `fees_paid_cents`, `fees_open_cents`), the shortfall (`shortfall_cents`, `shortfall_final`, `shortfall_due_on`, `shortfall_paid_on`), what the company owes (`company_cents`, `company_open_cents`) and the status (`paid` | `open` | `overdue` | `nothing_due`); `GET …/invoices/{period}` lists the lines. `POST …/invoices/{period}/pay {"paid_on"}` (staff, from the month's first day) registers at once every open fee of the month when the company pays them (taxes, the employees' "payment received" and plan status follow, as for a single payment) and, once the month ended, its shortfall; the company gets the receipt. With `payer: employee` the company's invoice carries only the shortfall.

**Quote.** `GET /subscriptions/quote?customer_id&modality_id&agreement_id[&joined_on&expires_on]` (staff) says whether the enrollment is allowed (`allowed`, `reason`), the conditions (`negotiated_discount_percent`, `grace_days`, `payer`, `agreement_ends_on`) and the headcount of the join month counting it (`employees`, `min_employees`, `conditions_met`); the subscription form shows it.

**Notices.** When an agreement reaches its minimum for the current month, or falls below it, the staff (in-app) and the company (its portal logins and contact email) are told (`agreement.conditions`); a new month or a cancellation taking effect is caught by the worker on its next pass (`watch_agreements`; told once). Paid invoices send the company a receipt (`agreement.invoice_paid`), and the staff is told when a company enrolls or removes an employee in its portal (`agreement.enrollment`).

### Co-payment

In a co-payment agreement (`payer: shared`) **every fee is split** between the company — `company_share_percent` of it, rounded half up — and the employee, who pays the rest. The contract sets the **default split** (`Agreement.company_share_percent`, 1–99, or a plan's own `AgreementPlan.company_share_percent`), frozen with the other terms. The split of each employee is then **agreed between the company and the employee** and may change over time:

* on enrollment (`company_share_percent` on `POST /subscriptions` — staff — or `POST /portal/employees` — the company; default the plan's or the agreement's split);
* later, **from a date on**, for one employee (`PUT /subscriptions/{id}/share` — staff, any date, on the company's word — or `PUT /portal/employees/{id}/share` — the company, from today on) or for every employee of an agreement (`PUT /agreements/{id}/share`, `PUT /portal/agreements/{id}/share`), with an optional note.

Each change is a row of the enrollment's **split history** (`enrollment_shares`: percent, effective from, note, who, when; `share_history` on the subscription and the portal's employee): the row in force on the first day of a fee's period splits that fee. A change re-splits the **open fees** from its date on; a fee with **a part already paid keeps its split**. The employee is told of every change (`agreement.share_changed`, with what each side pays a month from then on), and the staff when the company made it. An upgrade inside the same agreement and a renewal carry the employee's agreed split over. In `company` and `employee` agreements the split is fixed (100 % / 0 %: `409`).

**Two parts, two payments.** Every corporate fee records the company's part (`company_share_cents`) and the employee's (the rest), each paid on its own: `company_paid_on` / `employee_paid_on` (and cents, and who registered it). The company's part is paid through its **monthly invoice** (`POST /agreements/{id}/invoices/{period}/pay` registers the company's part of every fee of the month, plus the shortfall); the employee's part on the subscription (`PUT /subscriptions/{id}/installments/{iid}` with `part: "employee"` — or `"company"`; omitted, every part still open; `paid_on: null` undoes one part, or both). **The fee is paid once both parts are in** (a part of 0 needs no payment): only then its status, the plan's status and coverage, its taxes and the "payment received" notice follow (`paid_on` is the later part, `paid_cents` both); a part paid alone notifies the employee (`installment.part_paid`) with what is still open. So an employee whose part is overdue has an overdue plan, like anyone. A company-paid fee is simply one whose employee part is 0, and an employee-paid one whose company part is 0 — the same machinery serves the three modes. The company's invoice carries **its part only** (`company_share_cents`, `company_fees_paid_cents`, `company_fees_open_cents`, `company_cents`, `company_open_cents`), with the employees' parts alongside for the record (`employee_share_cents`, `employee_paid_cents`, `employee_open_cents`); each line shows both parts.

**Statements: what each side pays.**

* **The company's statement** — `GET /portal/statement` (its portal, UI "Statement") and `GET /partners/{id}/statement` (the staff, from the partner page), with `start` / `end` (`YYYY-MM`, default this year) and `agreement_id`: month by month (employees billed, fees, the company's part, the employees' parts, the shortfall, what the company paid and owes, what the employees paid and owe) and employee by employee (the split in force today, months billed, what each side pays, the employee's paid / open), by the month the fees are due. `…/statement.csv` is the **payroll list**: one row per employee fee — month, agreement, employee, CPF, plan, installment, due date, fee, the company's percentage, the company's part and its payment, the employee's part and its payment — for the company to deduct each employee's part.
* **The employee's statement** — `GET /reports/employee-statement` (the client's own, UI "My payments"; the staff any customer's with `customer_id`, from the customer page → "Payments"), `start` / `end`: the customer's **own part** of every fee due in the months — all of it for an individual plan, its share in a co-payment, nothing when its company pays —, what its company covers and has paid, and its paid / open / overdue totals.

**Money reports follow the cash.** Plan fees are counted as received **on each part's payment date** (the finance report's `plan_payments` / `plan_revenue_cents`, the plans report, the admin dashboard), while a fee's taxes are due once it is settled (both parts in). The plans report's `by_agreement` adds what was received from the company and from the employees (`company_paid_cents`, `employee_paid_cents`).

### The companies' self-service portal

Each partner company can have **portal logins** (role `partner`, created at `/users` by administrators and supervisors, linked to the company). A company login reaches **its portal and nothing else** of the network (no customers, appointments, catalog, professionals, reports…: `403`); deactivating the company revokes its logins at once (`401`). In the portal (`GET /portal…`, UI: "Company portal"):

* **Home** (`GET /portal`): the company, its agreements with the headcount of this month against the minimum, employees enrolled today, what it still owes and the shortfalls to pay.
* **Agreements** (`GET /portal/agreements[/{id}]`, `…/{id}/invoices[/{period}]`): the frozen terms, the minimum month by month and its invoices (read-only: payments are registered by Santé Saúde). Another company's agreement is `404`.
* **Employees** (`GET /portal/employees[?agreement_id]`, `GET /portal/employees/{id}`): its enrollments — name, CPF, birth date, contact, plan, period, monthly fee, dependents count, status and the **waiting period** that applies to the employee (`grace_days`: the agreement's, else the plan category's, else the plan's — "None" when waived; UI column "Waiting period"). **Never health data.**
* **Enroll** (`POST /portal/employees`: `agreement_id`, `modality_id`, `name`, `document` (CPF, required), optional `birth_date`, `email`, `phone`, `joined_on` — today or later, default today or the contract's start — and, in a co-payment, the `company_share_percent` agreed with the employee): the plan runs until the end of the contract, under the same rules as the staff's enrollments. The person is found by **CPF and name** — an existing customer keeps its record untouched, and a CPF registered under another name is refused, so a CPF alone reveals nobody — or registered with what the company gave. Someone already enrolled, or with a personal plan for the period, is refused (`409`: Santé Saúde moves a personal plan into the agreement). The company can enroll its employees in the next contract while the current one runs.
* **Edit** (`PUT /portal/employees/{id}`: `name`, `document` (CPF, required), `birth_date`, `email`, `phone`, optional `modality_id`, `joined_on`): the company corrects its employee's identification and contact (a CPF registered to another person is `409`), moves it to another plan of the agreement (the same rules as the staff's edit: the open fees follow the new plan's negotiated conditions; the headcount is re-checked) or — only before the enrollment starts and with nothing paid — changes its start (today or later, before the contract's end; otherwise `409`).
* **Dependents** (`GET|POST /portal/employees/{id}/dependents`, `PUT|DELETE /portal/employees/{id}/dependents/{dependent_id}`: `name`, `birth_date` (required), `kinship`, optional `document` (CPF) and `sex`): the company adds, corrects and removes its employee's dependents. Only identification is exchanged — a dependent's health data (allergies, conditions, medications, notes) is neither shown nor touched by the company. The open fees follow at once: each dependent above the plan's `dependent_limit` costs its `extra_dependent_cents`, with the agreement's discount (of the enrollment and, if different, of the employee's plan in force). A dependent with appointments is kept for its history (`409`: Santé Saúde removes it).
* Edits and dependents are possible while the enrollment is active (a cancelled or ended enrollment is `409`), and the **employee is told** of every change made by its company (`agreement.employee_updated`, listing what changed).
* **Remove** (`POST /portal/employees/{id}/cancel`, optional `cancelled_on`, today or later): an employee who left; the fees of the periods starting after that day are no longer owed. An enrollment that has not started (and has nothing paid) is withdrawn altogether.
* **Co-payment** (`PUT /portal/employees/{id}/share`, `PUT /portal/agreements/{id}/share`): the split agreed with an employee, or with all of them, from today on.
* **Statement** (`GET /portal/statement`, `/portal/statement.csv`): what the company and its employees pay, and the payroll list.

Agreements show their status (`scheduled` | `in_force` | `ended` | `suspended`), the headcount of the reference month (`reference_period`, `employees`, `conditions_met`, `missing_employees`), the projected `shortfall_cents` and `shortfall_open_cents` (ended months not paid yet), and their employees (`GET /subscriptions?agreement_id=` or `?partner_id=`). `GET /reports/plans` adds `by_agreement` (employees this month against the minimum, enrollments, fees and discount, shortfalls received in the range and still to collect); the finance report counts the shortfalls as plan-fee revenue (`agreement_charges`, `agreement_charges_cents`) and the tax report/ledger their taxes; the staff dashboard counts the agreements in force and those below their minimum this month. Rules live in `api/app/partners.py`; the endpoints in `api/app/routers/partners.py` (staff) and `api/app/routers/portal.py` (companies). UI: "Corporate" → "Partner companies" / "Agreements" (terms, renewal, employees, the company's invoices), the "Corporate agreement" field of the subscription form, and the company portal.

## Patient verification: the virtual card

To make sure the person in the room is the patient, every patient — the holder and each dependent — has a **virtual card** with a **6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds**, like a second authentication factor. The code is a TOTP (RFC 6238: HMAC-SHA1 over the 30-second step, 6 digits) computed by the server from a secret that never leaves it (`card_secret`, issued on first use). Only the patient's **own client login** can see the card (`GET /customers/{id}/card`, `GET /customers/{id}/dependents/{depId}/card` → name, card number `SS 000123 00`, plan, the code, `seconds_remaining`); the staff gets `403`, so nobody but the patient can present it. The UI shows it at `/my-card` with a countdown and fetches the next code when the current one expires.

**Starting the visit.** The reception or the appointment's professional types the code the patient shows: `POST /appointments/{id}/start` with `{"code": "123 456"}`. The server checks it against the secret of the appointment's patient (the dependent's card when the appointment is for a dependent), accepting the **current code and the previous one** (one 30-second step back, never a future one; constant-time comparison). A wrong code is `400` and counted; after **5** wrong codes the appointment is **locked for 5 minutes** (`429`) and the patient and the staff are alerted (`card.locked`). Starting is allowed from the check-in window (2 h before the slot) while the appointment is scheduled, registers the arrival when there was no check-in (`attendance=in_progress`), and notifies the patient ("card verified") so a leaked code is noticed at once. The staff can undo a start registered on the wrong appointment (`DELETE …/start`, before the signature). The patient or the staff can **reset** a card (`POST …/card/reset`): a new secret is issued, old codes stop working, the patient is notified. Rules live in `api/app/virtual_card.py`.

## Professional's signature (completion)

An appointment is **completed** (`realizada`) only when the professional who attended it **signs** it: `POST /appointments/{id}/sign`, allowed only for the `professional` login tied to the appointment's professional, **after the visit was started with the patient's card code**, after the slot started, never on a cancelled appointment and never twice. The chain is therefore: patient verified (card) → visit → professional signs → payout.

**A signature is three factors in one request** (`api/app/signing.py`), for appointments and for payout receipts alike: the body carries `password` (the account password typed again), `code` (a 6-digit **TOTP** from the professional's authenticator app — current or previous 30-second code) and `signature_image` (the **hand-drawn signature** from the signature pad, a JPEG data URL up to 300 KB, at least 50×20 px), plus optional `notes`. The drawing is stored with the signed record, shown in the UI and printed on the receipt PDF. Five wrong passwords/codes lock the account's signing for five minutes (`429`). Revoking a signature (`DELETE …/sign` with `password` and `code`) asks for the first two factors again.

**Authenticator enrolment.** Each professional sets up its app once: `POST /auth/totp/setup` returns the base32 key and the `otpauth://` URI (typed or pasted into Google/Microsoft Authenticator, Authy…), `POST /auth/totp/confirm {"code"}` activates it after a first valid code, `GET /auth/totp` reports the status; `/auth/me` carries `totp_enabled`. Nothing can be signed before the authenticator is confirmed (`403`), and the UI (`/my-security`, plus a banner on the professional's home) guides the setup. Lost phone: an administrator resets it (`POST /users/{id}/totp/reset`) and the professional enrols again. The demo login `lorem.ipsum@example.com` comes with the RFC 6238 test key `GEZD GNBV GY3T QOJQ GEZD GNBV GY3T QOJQ` already confirmed. Nobody else — reception, supervisor, administrator or patient — can mark an appointment as completed: a create/edit body with `status=realizada` is refused with `403` unless the appointment is already signed. The check-out records that the visit ended and leaves the appointment **awaiting signature** (`awaiting_signature=true`; the dashboard counts them and the professional's home lists them under "To sign").

The professional may revoke its own signature (`DELETE /appointments/{id}/sign`: back to `agendada`, out of the payout). On a signed appointment the patient, professional and time are frozen (`409`) — the staff can still edit the notes or cancel it, which drops the signature. Every appointment exposes `signed_at`, `signed_by_name`, `awaiting_signature` and, for the current user, `can_sign` / `can_revoke_signature`. Rules live in `api/app/signature.py`.

The **professional role** sees only its own appointments (`GET /appointments` is scoped; other professionals' appointments are `403`; `start`/`end` narrow the list to a range of days), has no access to customers, plans or the management area, organises its work in **My agenda** (`/my-agenda`: the visit under way and the next one at a glance — a started or checked-in visit, else the slot that contains the current time —, the **working week as a grid** by default — one column per day with slots or appointments, one row per slot, free / booked / time off / on-call cells, a line at the current time — and a **month view** with every day's appointments; built from `GET /appointments?start&end`, `GET /professionals/{id}/slots` and the time off), gets in-app notifications about its agenda (new / moved / cancelled appointments, patient checked in, visit awaiting signature) and follows its own payout (`/my-payouts` in the UI: `GET /reports/payouts`, `/reports/payouts/{ownId}` and `/reports/payouts.csv` scoped to itself). Administrators create professional logins at `/users` (role "Professional" + the professional record).

## Chat of remote appointments (end-to-end encrypted)

Every **remote appointment** has a chat between the **patient** (the customer's client logins) and the **appointment's professional**, on the appointment page, so complaints and evidence (photos, PDFs, documents, audio) can be shared before, during and after the video call. It is **end-to-end encrypted** (`api/app/chat.py`, `front/src/chatCrypto.js`): the server relays ciphertext and never holds a key, so the staff — who may read the appointment — cannot read the chat (`403` on every chat endpoint; the UI shows only that it exists).

How it works (protocol "sante-saude chat v1", all with WebCrypto in the browser): each user has an **identity key pair** (ECDH P-256) generated by its browser — the private key is non-extractable and stays in the device's IndexedDB, the public key is published (`PUT /chat/key`, JWK, with its SHA-256 fingerprint). Each conversation has a random **AES-256-GCM key** generated by the party who opens the chat first and **wrapped for every participant's public key** (ephemeral ECDH + HKDF-SHA256 + AES-GCM envelope, `POST /appointments/{id}/chat/keys`; an envelope is never replaced and goes to participants' current keys only). Messages are AES-GCM ciphertext of `{text, attachments[]}` bound to the conversation; attachments are AES-GCM blobs uploaded before the message (`POST …/chat/attachments`, up to `CHAT_ATTACHMENT_MAX_BYTES` each, 5 per message) whose names, types, sizes and IVs travel *inside* the encrypted message — the server knows sender, time, blob ids and sizes only, so the only rules it can enforce are sizes and counts (the UI restricts the picker). A **safety code** (hash of both public keys, the same on both screens) is shown under "Security details" to be compared over the video call: equal codes mean nobody swapped a key. A user on a **new device** (or after clearing the browser) gets a new identity; `GET …/chat` tells the other party who still lacks an envelope (`keys_pending`) and its browser wraps the key again on its next visit — until then the newcomer waits (the server will not accept messages or envelopes from someone without the key). The conversation key being the same, the whole history opens again.

Endpoints (`require_appointment_area`, participants only): `GET /appointments/{id}/chat` (state, participants with fingerprints and whether each has an envelope, `my_key`, `keys_pending`, `safety_code`, unread count, limits), `POST …/chat/keys`, `GET …/chat/messages?after=` (polling), `POST …/chat/messages`, `POST …/chat/attachments`, `GET …/chat/attachments/{aid}`, `POST …/chat/read`. The chat is **open** while the appointment is not cancelled and until **30 days after the visit**, read-only afterwards (`409` to post). A new message notifies the other side (`chat.message`, in-app and by email, **no content**), at most once every 30 minutes per side. Attachments follow the documentation retention (purged with the prescriptions after 10 years, messages stay as ciphertext); blobs uploaded but never sent are purged after a day; a hard-deleted appointment takes its chat along. The API's test double of the browser (`api/tests/chat_client.py`) implements the same protocol, and a fixture it generates is opened by the JavaScript implementation in the front-end unit tests — the two sides are cross-checked.

## Medical prescriptions

An appointment may carry **up to 5 prescription files** (`api/app/prescriptions.py`, router `api/app/routers/prescriptions.py`; the "Prescriptions" panel on the appointment page). Only **text documents** are accepted — PDF, plain text (`.txt`), RTF, Word (`.doc`/`.docx`) and OpenDocument text (`.odt`) — and the type is decided by **sniffing the content**, never by the file name or the declared content type (a JPEG renamed `.pdf` is `415`); the stored name takes the extension of the detected type; up to `PRESCRIPTION_MAX_BYTES` per file (default 10 MB, `413`); a sixth file is `409`.

* **Who attaches and removes**: the staff and the appointment's professional (`POST /appointments/{id}/prescriptions`, multipart `file` + optional `note`; `DELETE …/prescriptions/{docId}`). Nothing can be attached to a cancelled appointment (`409`). Patients only read.
* **Who reads**: everyone who may read the appointment — the staff (administrators, supervisors, attendants), the patient's client login and the appointment's professional; **patients only see signed prescriptions** (the valid ones — see below), the staff and the professional also see the drafts awaiting the signature. `GET /appointments/{id}/prescriptions` lists the files (name, type, size, SHA-256, who attached it and when, `retain_until`) and gives each one a **signed download link** (`download_url`, `link_expires_at`): the link is the credential — it opens in the browser without the bearer token — and **expires `PRESCRIPTION_LINK_DAYS` after it was issued, 30 days at most** (`403` afterwards; reopening the appointment issues fresh links). It is bound to the file's fingerprint, so a removed file's links stop working at once, and a tampered token is refused. `GET …/prescriptions/{docId}/download` also accepts the bearer of someone with access. Every appointment carries `prescriptions_count`.
* **Lifecycle**: **cancelling** an appointment (or deleting it) **deletes its files at once** — content and records; re-activating it does not bring them back. The documentation is kept for the **legal retention of 10 years** counted from the date of the visit (`retain_until`, following the appointment if it is moved); the worker purges what is older on every pass (`purge_expired`, content included).
* Files live in `PRESCRIPTIONS_DIR` (default `api/prescriptions`, outside the database and the repository) under random names. The professional is notified when someone else attaches a prescription (`appointment.prescription`: awaiting its signature); the patient when the prescription is signed (`appointment.prescription_signed`, in-app and by email).

**Digital signature (ICP-Brasil).** A prescription is valid for pharmacies only once **the appointment's professional signs it** — nobody else can, not the staff, not another professional (`403`). The professional signs from the appointment page with its **ICP-Brasil certificate** — an A1 file (`.pfx`/`.p12`) and its password, sent with the request (`POST /appointments/{id}/prescriptions/{docId}/sign`, multipart `certificate` + `password`) and **used in memory only: the private key is never stored**. The system produces a **CAdES-BES detached signature** (`.p7s`: CMS/RFC 5652 + ETSI EN 319 122-1, SHA-256 with RSA, the signed attributes `content-type`, `message-digest`, `signing-time` and `signing-certificate-v2`, the signer's certificate and chain embedded — the format ITI's *Verificador de Conformidade* and OpenSSL accept) over the exact bytes of the file, and **verifies it before accepting it** (`api/app/cms.py`, an independent CMS parser/verifier): the certificate chain must reach a root of the **trust bundle** (`ICP_BRASIL_TRUST_BUNDLE`: a PEM file with the ICP-Brasil root CAs published by ITI — without it nothing can be signed, `503`), every certificate must be valid at the signing time, and the certificate's **CPF** (ICP-Brasil person `otherName`, OID 2.16.76.1.3.1) must be the CPF on the professional's record (`professionals.document`, set by the staff on the professional form — without it the professional cannot sign, `409`; another person's certificate is `403`). An untrusted, expired or unreadable certificate, or a wrong password, is `400`. Signing twice or on a cancelled appointment is `409`. When the ICP-Brasil **AD-RB signature policy** is configured (`ICP_BRASIL_POLICY_OID`, `_URL`, `_SHA256`) every signature references it (`signature-policy-identifier`). The signature snapshots the signer (name, CPF, certificate issuer, serial, expiry, the public certificate) on the file.

**Verification.** `GET …/prescriptions/{docId}/signature` re-verifies the stored file and `.p7s` now and reports every check (`structure`, `signer-certificate`, `algorithms`, `content-type`, `message-digest`, `signing-time`, `signing-certificate-v2`, `signature`, `chain`, `validity`, `stored-files`, `signer`): a file or signature altered on disk fails it. `GET …/prescriptions/{docId}/signature.p7s` downloads the detached signature (same signed link as the file), so the patient can hand the pharmacy both files. **Pharmacies** verify them without an account — `POST /prescriptions/verify` (multipart `file` + `signature`; UI at `/verify-prescription`, linked from the login page): the same report plus whether the signer is a professional of the network (`professional_registered`, name, specialty) — or with ITI's validator / `openssl cms -verify` against the ICP-Brasil roots. Revocation (CRL/OCSP) is not checked. For local trials without a real certificate, `scripts/make_demo_certificate.py` builds a demo PKI (`demo-ca/roots.pem` to use as the trust bundle, plus `.pfx` files for the seeded professionals, password `saude123`); the e2e suite uses it.

## Reports

`GET /reports/appointments`, `/reports/attendants`, `/reports/plans` with `granularity=week|month`, `start`, `end` (default: last 8 weeks / 6 months) and, for appointments and attendants, `professional_id` / `clinic_id`. Weeks are ISO weeks (`2026-W43`), months `2026-10`. Appointment amounts sum scheduled + completed appointments (cancelled are counted but add nothing); the ones booked over the plan limit are counted apart with what they bill at the full price (`over_limit`, `over_limit_cents`, per period and per professional/clinic/specialty/plan), and so are the on-call services (`on_call`, `on_call_cents`). Attendant efficiency counts appointments booked (by booking date), completion rate, plans sold and payments registered per user. Plan sales count subscriptions created and cancelled, payments received (by payment date), the current active/overdue/pending totals and, per plan and period, the appointments booked with it inside its allowance (`appointments_covered`) or over it (`appointments_over_limit`, `over_limit_cents`); `by_agreement` gives the corporate (B2B) side per agreement (see *Corporate partnerships*). The finance report separates the completed appointments billed at the full price (`appointments_over_limit`, `appointments_over_limit_cents`) and the payout statement/CSV flag them (`over_limit`; the professional's fee is the same either way) as well as the on-call services (`on_call`: paid at the on-call rate; the payout rows carry the professional's current `on_call_fee_cents` and the on-call totals). Aggregations live in `api/app/reports.py`.

## Professional payouts

`GET /reports/payouts` controls what the clinic owes each professional for a period. An appointment pays the professional **only when it is completed**, i.e. **signed by the professional** (`realizada`), and it pays the **professional's fee snapshotted when it was booked** (`professional_price_cents`), so later fee changes never rewrite what is due; the plan discount is borne by the network, not by the professional. Scheduled appointments are shown as "not due yet" and cancelled ones never pay. Appointments are bucketed by service date (`marked_at`).

Parameters: `granularity=week|month` (default month), `start`, `end` and the professional filters `name` (case-insensitive partial match), `specialty_id`, `clinic_id`, `professional_id`. The response has the totals, the amount per period, and one row per professional matching the filters (also those with nothing due, so the control is complete) with its own per-period amounts. `GET /reports/payouts/{professionalId}?start&end` is the **statement** backing the payment: every appointment of the professional in the range with its status, patient, plan, billed amount and the amount due; `GET /reports/payouts.csv` exports one row per paid appointment (same filters, with the signature time). Administrators see everyone (the "Professional payouts" tab of Reports); a professional login gets the same endpoints scoped to itself ("My payouts"). Aggregation lives in `api/app/reports.py` (`payout_cents`, `payouts_by_professional`).

## Paying professionals and clinics: advances, settlements, receipts

The network collects from patients and pays each **payee** its share of every signed appointment: the professional's fee (`professional_price_cents`) or the clinic's fee (`fee_cents`), both snapshotted at booking. Payment is rarely a single transfer, so the system models (`api/app/payouts.py`, router `/payouts`, UI at `/payouts` for administrators and inside "My payouts" for professionals):

* **Advances** (`POST /payouts/advances`): money handed ahead of any settlement, with its receipt issued at once. An advance is deducted from the next settlement(s); what does not fit carries over. It can be removed only while it was neither deducted nor confirmed.
* **Settlements** (`POST /payouts/settlements` with `until`, `installments` 1–12, `first_due_on`, `interval_days`): close the payee's account up to a cutoff date — every signed appointment not settled yet is gathered (`earned`), outstanding advances are applied (`advances`, oldest first) and the **net** is split into equal **installments** with due dates (the cents that do not divide go to the first one). A net of zero (fully covered by advances) needs no installment and is settled at once. Each appointment is settled once per payee (`professional_settlement_id` / `clinic_settlement_id`). A settlement nothing was paid on can be **voided**: its appointments and advances go back to pending. Status is derived: `open` → `awaiting_signature` (all paid, some receipts unconfirmed) → `settled`, or `void`.
* **Installment payments** (`PUT /payouts/settlements/{id}/installments/{paymentId}` with `paid_on`, `method` pix/transfer/cash/check/other, `reference`): each one issues a **receipt**.
* **Receipts**: every payment made gets a sequential number per year (`REC-2026-000017`), a **digital receipt** (`GET /payouts/receipts/{id}`: company header, payee, amount, date, method, who registered it, the settlement summary with its installments and deducted advances) and a **PDF** (`GET /payouts/receipts/{id}.pdf`, generated by `api/app/pdf.py`, no external library). A **fingerprint** (SHA-256 of the receipt's content and confirmation) is printed on both so a PDF can be checked against the system. **The professional always signs the receipt** (`POST /payouts/receipts/{id}/sign`, its own login only) to confirm the money was received; administrators are notified. Clinics have no login, so their receipts are **acknowledged** by an administrator recording who received the money and their document (`POST /payouts/receipts/{id}/acknowledge`).
* **Position** (`GET /payouts/position`): signed-not-settled earnings, advances outstanding, installments to receive, receipts to confirm, total received and the **balance** (unsettled + open installments − outstanding advances). Professionals see their own account (advances, settlements, receipts to sign) in "My payouts" and on their home; administrators pick the payee at `/payouts`.
* **Automatic monthly closing** (`api/app/closing.py`). On the closing day of each month (`SETTLEMENT_DAY`, default the 1st) the worker closes the **previous month**: a `PayoutClosing` row (unique per period, so it never runs twice) and one `payout.close` job are created; that job fans out one `payout.settle` job **per payee** (every professional and clinic), so one payee's failure never blocks the others and each is retried on its own. Each settle job is idempotent and creates the settlement with the standard plan: cutoff = last day of the month, installments growing with the net amount (one more per `SETTLEMENT_INSTALLMENT_STEP_CENTS`, default R$ 2.000, capped at `SETTLEMENT_MAX_INSTALLMENTS`, default 3), first due `SETTLEMENT_FIRST_DUE_DAYS` after the month end (default 5), then every `SETTLEMENT_INTERVAL_DAYS` (default 30). Payees with nothing to settle are skipped and counted. Administrators follow the closings at `/payouts` (`GET /payouts/closings`) and may close a month by hand (`POST /payouts/closings {"period": "2026-10"}`, same jobs, once per period). Manual settlements remain available for ad-hoc cases.

The professional is notified when an advance is registered, a settlement is created and an installment is paid (each asking for the signature); administrators when a receipt is signed.

**Reports.** `GET /reports/payouts` now carries, per professional and per period, the money actually handed over (advances, installments, total, receipts unconfirmed — by payment date) next to what was earned (by service date), plus the position today (unsettled, advances outstanding, open installments, balance). `GET /reports/finance` adds the payouts handed to professionals and clinics in the period and the **result after payouts** (`net − payouts`). The dashboard shows the month's payouts and unconfirmed receipts (admin) and the balance to receive and receipts to sign (professional).

## Taxes and accounting

Taxes over the services are computed from **tax rules** (`api/app/taxes.py`, managed at `/tax-rules`; staff read, **only administrators write**). A rule has a tax code (ISS, PIS, COFINS…), a level (federal / state / municipal), a rate in basis points (1 bp = 0.01 %), what it applies to (appointments, plan fees or both), a calculation base (billed amount after the plan discount, or gross price), a validity period and optional scope filters: **state, municipality, clinic, specialty, plan**. For each code, the most specific active rule whose filters all match the service applies (newest wins on ties), so a default municipal ISS can be overridden for one city, one clinic or one specialty.

Clinics carry their municipality, state, IBGE code and CNPJ; the **Company** settings (`/company`) hold the head office used as the jurisdiction for plan fees and as the header of the accounting reports.

Tax lines are **snapshotted** on each appointment when it is priced (`tax_cents`, `net_cents`, `taxes[]`) and on each installment when its payment is registered, so later rule changes never rewrite history; re-pricing (other professional/customer) or moving an appointment to another date re-applies the rules in force on the service date. Rules already referenced by stored lines are deactivated instead of deleted. `GET /tax-rules/preview` simulates a service.

Reports (admin): `GET /reports/taxes` — taxes per period, by tax code, level, **jurisdiction** (federal / state / municipality with IBGE code) and source (appointments vs plan fees), on services rendered (`basis=completed`, default) or including scheduled ones (`basis=all`); `GET /reports/taxes/ledger.csv` — one row per tax line for the accountant; `GET /reports/finance` — revenue recognised (completed appointments + plan fees received), discounts granted, taxes and net per period.

## Addresses and maps

Clinics and the company carry a full postal address (street, number, complement, district, municipality, state, ZIP), phone, "how to get there" notes and **coordinates**. Address forms (clinics, company, customers, dependents) share one block with **ZIP lookup** (ViaCEP) and **"Find on map"** geocoding (OpenStreetMap Nominatim); both are optional helpers that need internet access from the browser. The API returns a formatted `address`, a `maps_url` (Google Maps, by coordinates when known) and an `osm_url` (`api/app/addresses.py`).

Every appointment exposes the clinic's address, phone, directions, coordinates and map link; the appointment page shows a **map** (OpenStreetMap embed, no dependency) with Google Maps / Waze / OpenStreetMap route links, the lists and the dashboard show "Directions", and every appointment email and reminder includes the address and the map link. A **remote appointment** shows its video-call link ("Join the call") in place of all of that.

## Attendance (check-in / check-out)

To audit that appointments really happen, the patient — or the reception on their behalf — confirms **arrival** (`POST /appointments/{id}/check-in`, allowed from 2 h before to 2 h after the slot, configurable), the visit is **started** with the patient's card code (above, `attendance=in_progress`), and the **end of the visit** is confirmed with the check-out (`POST /appointments/{id}/check-out`). All record who did it and when (and, when the browser allows it, the device position). Staff may undo a mistaken check-in (`DELETE …/check-in`) while there is no check-out and no start. The check-out does **not** complete the appointment: it leaves it awaiting the professional's signature (see above). A scheduled appointment whose window passed without a check-in is a **no-show** (derived, `attendance=no_show`). Rules live in `api/app/attendance.py`.

The check-in notifies the patient (receipt), the staff (who is waiting, for which professional) and the professional; the check-out sends the patient a receipt and asks the professional to sign; the signature triggers the completion notification. `GET /reports/attendance` (admin, supervisor) gives, per period, clinic and professional: scheduled, checked in, checked out, completed without check-in, no-shows, awaiting, cancelled, attendance rate, average arrival offset and visit duration; the dashboard shows today's check-ins and the month's no-shows.

## Dashboard

`/` is a role-aware home (`GET /dashboard`): appointments today / next 7 days / completed and cancelled this month, visits awaiting signature, next appointments, plan counts (active, overdue, pending), installments due in 7 days and the corporate agreements in force / below their minimum of employees this month for staff; the company portal's own home for a partner company's login; month revenue, discounts, taxes, net and delivery-queue health for administrators; own appointments and plan for clients; own agenda, the appointments to sign, the month's signed count and payout for professionals.

**Queue, broker and retries** (`api/app/queue.py`, `api/app/broker.py`). Every job (emails, the monthly payout closing and its per-payee settlements) is a `queue_jobs` row written in the same transaction as the event — a **transactional outbox**, so a job is never lost between "it happened" and "the message exists". The worker **relays** due jobs to the message broker and marks the row `queued` only after the broker **confirmed** the message; **consumers** take the messages, run the job and **acknowledge** only after its outcome was committed, so a consumer that dies mid-job has its message redelivered (handlers are idempotent; a duplicate of a `done` job is acked and skipped). A failed handler puts the job back to `pending` with exponential back-off (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 min… capped at 6 h) up to `max_attempts` (5), then parks it as **dead**; a `queued` row nobody consumed for 30 min is published again. Messages that point nowhere are rejected to the **dead-letter queue**. `BROKER_URL=memory` (default, development and tests) keeps everything in process with the same contract; `BROKER_URL=amqp://user:pass@host:5672/%2F` uses **RabbitMQ** through `pika` (durable exchange `sante` and queue `sante.jobs` with dead-letter exchange `sante.dlx` → `sante.jobs.dead`, persistent messages, publisher confirms, `prefetch` per consumer). With RabbitMQ, run the API with `WORKER_ENABLED=0` and as many worker processes as needed: `python -m app.worker`. Administrators see the queue at `/queue` (stats including the broker depth, per-job error, "Retry now", "Retry all dead", "Run worker now"). Mail backends: `console`, `file`, `smtp`, `memory` (see `.env.example`).

**Reminders.** The worker stamps `reminded_day_at` / `reminded_hour_at` on the appointment when it creates each reminder, so they fire exactly once; moving an appointment resets the stamps. Only `agendada` appointments are reminded.

**UI.** The upper right of the topbar holds the notification bell and, beside it, **Sign out** (for every role). The bell polls `/notifications/summary` (badge with the unread count, latest messages, mark as read, toast for new arrivals); `/notifications` lists everything. Every API request result is reported by a toast: errors always, successes for create/update/delete.

* **Unit** (`npm run test:unit`, Node's built-in runner, no browser): the role permissions the UI mirrors, the agenda helpers — current / next visit, week grid — and the chat encryption (round trips, and the fixture produced by the API's Python double of the browser) (`tests/unit/`).
* **End-to-end** (`npm run test:e2e`, Playwright, `e2e/`): real browser against the real front-end and API. `playwright.config.js` starts the API on port 8000 with its own seeded database (`api/e2e.db`) and the Vite dev server on 5173; each spec builds its own scenario through the API (a professional working all day, a covered customer, appointments in the past or the future) and drives the UI: the professional's area and guards, listing and filtering its agenda, signing / revoking and the resulting payout, the staff side of the signature (no "completed" by hand, check-out leaves it awaiting signature), the creation of professional logins, the virtual card (the patient's rotating code and countdown, dependents' cards, the reception starting the visit with the code — wrong code refused —, the professional starting and only then signing, the "card used" notification and the card reset), the payouts (the admin registering an advance, creating a settlement in installments and paying one; the professional signing the receipts — password, authenticator code, drawing on the signature pad — and downloading the PDF with the drawing; a clinic's receipt acknowledged; supervisors kept out), the authenticator enrolment through the UI (the test computes the TOTP like an app would) and the monthly closing triggered by hand and carried through by the worker, the sales regions (the reception recording a seller's sale bound by the region, a supervisor setting a region and a seller up, the client never seeing the commission) and the seller's area (registering a customer and recording a sale, the out-of-region block, a change request confirmed by the client, the lock-out of a deactivated seller, a coordinator's team page and override, a plan upgrade from the subscription form, the commission report and notifications, the account and password, the areas it never reaches) the remote appointments (the professional offering them in its profile, the reception booking a video call, the link shown to the reception, the professional and the patient) the professional's agenda (the visit under way and the next one, the week grid, the month view, the navigation; kept from the staff) and the prescriptions (the professional attaching a PDF and signing it with the demo certificate — wrong password refused —, the patient downloading the signed prescription and its `.p7s`, an image refused, a pharmacy verifying the pair on the public page) and the encrypted chat (the patient writing and attaching a photo, the professional reading and replying, equal safety codes, the staff seeing nothing) and the corporate partnerships (a supervisor registering a company and negotiating its six-month agreement, the reception enrolling employees at the negotiated price while the form tells when the company is below its minimum, the company's invoice paid at once, the employee's read-only corporate plan, sellers kept out) and the company portal (the company's login enrolling an employee, following its agreement, the projected shortfall and its invoices, removing the employee, the network's pages out of reach; the waiting-period column, editing the employee, adding, correcting and removing dependents with the fee following the plan's extra-dependent price, signing out from the topbar) and the co-payment (the company agreeing a split with its employee and changing it, the staff registering the company's part, the employee's "My payments", the company's statement) and the plan categories (a supervisor creating an enterprise category with its own waiting period, the reception unable to sell its plan to an individual but enrolling an employee in it, clients never seeing it). First run: `npx playwright install chromium`. Set `E2E_NO_SERVER=1` with `E2E_BASE_URL` / `E2E_API_URL` to test servers you already run.
* `npm test` runs both.
